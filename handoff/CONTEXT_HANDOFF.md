### The meta-lesson that emerged

**A business a person with no capital, no credentials, and no track record can start is,
almost by definition, a business with weak defensibility.** Those are the same property
viewed from two sides. The only thing that breaks the symmetry is an unfair advantage.
I have exactly one — the network in Part 2 — and every time I route away from it, I end
up somewhere I'm a stranger competing on credentials I don't have.

### The test to apply to any new idea

Before anything else: **what is one customer worth per year, and how many can I
realistically reach?** Multiply. If the answer isn't the business I want, stop there.
This single question would have saved three weeks on idea #6.

## PART 4 — VERIFIED RESEARCH FINDINGS

### Healthcare market structure
- Independent US physicians fell to **120,900** by January 2026. **82%** of physicians
  are now employed by hospitals or corporate entities. Physician-owned practices are
  **36.1%** of all practices, down from 70%+ a decade ago. ~81,100 fewer independent
  practices than in 2018. **My addressable market is shrinking every year.**
- Billing companies charge **4–9% of collections** (5–8% typical for small/mid).
- MGMA: physician groups lose **7–11% of net revenue** to underpaid and unpaid claims.
- Industry analyses: **5–30% of commercial claims paid incorrectly**; practices that
  look recover **0.8–2.0% of net revenue**.
- Appeal windows: **30–90 days from remittance date** (Medicare redetermination: 120).
- Cardiology median compensation ~$520k (MGMA). Procedural specialties collect roughly
  2–2.5x compensation.

### Competitive landscape for underpayment detection
- **MD Clarity** (closest competitor): **$3.1M ARR, 28–30 employees, bootstrapped.**
  Roughly $107k revenue per employee — services-heavy, hard to scale. **Nobody has
  scaled the small-practice segment after 15 years.**
- MD Clarity implementation costs **$1,000–20,000**, plus data migration $500–10,000,
  plus training $1,000–5,000 — which contradicts their "small practice" positioning.
- **The dead zone:** claims between $1,000 and $50,000 are declined by agencies and
  lawyers because fees exceed recovery. Small practices' entire annual underpayment
  often sits inside it. **The bottom of the market is unserved because it is
  uneconomic to serve with a salesforce** — which also constrains me.
- **athenahealth already flags underpayments** against a *loaded* fee schedule. The
  feature exists but sits dormant because nobody maintains the fee schedules. Never
  tell an athena practice their system can't do this — ask when they last updated it.

### RPM / CCM (Medicare remote care)
- 2026 Physician Fee Schedule created **CPT 99445** (device supply, 2–15 days) and
  **99470** (10–20 min management) — breaking the old 16-day and 20-minute thresholds
  that made this uneconomic for small practices.
- 2026 national rates: 99453 ~$22 (once), 99454 $52.11/mo, 99445 $52.11/mo,
  99457 $51.77/mo, 99470 $26.05/mo, 99458 $41.00/mo, **99490 $66.13/mo (CCM)**,
  99439 ~$50.44/mo.
- A fully enrolled patient bills roughly **$170/month**.
- **CCM reaches only ~4% of eligible Medicare beneficiaries**; practices that actively
  manage enrollment reach **30–35%**.
- RPM: Medicare paid **$536M in 2024, up 31%**, ~1M beneficiaries. **OIG has flagged it
  for enhanced oversight and announced Part B audits.**
- Devices cost **$120–350 per patient**; implementation $2,000–10,000. **CCM requires
  no hardware** — this is why CCM-first drops startup capital from ~$60k to ~$25k.
- **LEGAL, IMPORTANT:** Percentage-of-revenue arrangements ("70/30 revenue share") are
  **incompatible with Anti-Kickback Statute safe harbors** and many state fee-splitting
  laws. The compliant structure is a **flat per-patient-per-month fee at fair market
  value** ($40–80 PPPM full service; $30–40 CCM-only).
- CMS permits monitoring codes to be furnished by **clinical staff under general
  supervision** — the physician need not be present or reachable. This is the legal
  mechanism the entire enablement industry rests on.
- Evidence: CMS claims data shows CCM reduced hospitalizations **4.7%** and costs
  **$888/beneficiary/year**. (Vendor-published figures like "36%" should be discounted.)
- CCM carries **~$13/month patient coinsurance**, though many patients have Medigap,
  Medicaid, or retiree coverage that eliminates it.

### Document accessibility (ADA / Section 504)
- **DOJ Title II:** extended April 20, 2026 by one year → **April 26, 2027** (entities
  serving 50,000+) and **April 26, 2028** (under 50,000, plus **all special districts**).
- **HHS Section 504:** extended May 7, 2026 by one year → **May 11, 2027** (15+
  employees) and **May 10, 2028** (under 15). **Medicare Part B reimbursement alone
  triggers coverage**, sweeping in essentially every physician practice.
- **DOJ's stated reason for extending: "the limits of current technology, including
  generative AI, to automate accessibility remediation at scale."** A regulator
  describing an unmet service need in writing — and a warning against anyone promising
  full automation.
- The broader non-discrimination obligation has been in force since **July 8, 2024**.
  The deadline governs the technical standard, not the underlying duty.
- **Exceptions (narrowly construed):** archived web content; preexisting conventional
  electronic documents *unless currently used to access a service*; third-party content;
  preexisting social media; password-protected individualized documents. **Realistically
  only 25–60% of a document library is in scope.**
- Manual remediation: **$5–25/page** ($40–60 for complex scans). AI-assisted tooling:
  **$0.10–0.50/page**, ~95% auto-tagging, ~80% manual effort reduction. Human review
  still required for reading order, alt-text meaning, semantic tags.
- **The tools already exist and are cheap** — PDFix Desktop is ~$599/year. The business
  is the service, not the software.
- **accessiBe fined $1M by the FTC** (January 2025) for claiming automatic WCAG
  compliance. 800+ businesses using overlays were sued anyway. Courts reject overlays as
  evidence of compliance. **The market is hostile to automation marketing.**
- **90,837 local governments** in the US: 3,031 counties, 35,705 municipalities/
  townships, 12,546 school districts, 39,555 special districts. Prospect list is public.

## PART 5 — WHAT WAS ACTUALLY BUILT

A working payer underpayment detection system. **~3,700 lines of Python, zero external
dependencies.** Repository: `tekin8116/Business-Idea-2`, branch
`claude/sellable-product-idea-di2agb`.

### What it does
Ingests X12 835 electronic remittance advice files (the standard format every medical
clearinghouse can export) and identifies claim lines where an insurer allowed less than
it should have.

### Architecture
```
.835 files
   └─ x12/tokenizer.py    delimiter-aware X12 tokenizer
   └─ x12/parser835.py    assembles claims + service lines; skips patient identity
   └─ model.py            domain model; allowed-vs-paid logic
   └─ detect/             THE ENGINE
        grouping.py       decides what is comparable to what
        baseline.py       rate baselines + dated rate-change detection
        bundling.py       adjustment reason code review, add-on code misfires
        contract.py       comparison against contracted rates / % of Medicare
   └─ analysis.py         orchestration + deduplication
   └─ findings.py         appeal deadlines, ranking
   └─ report/             HTML report, CSV worklist, appeal letters
```

### Key design decisions
- **Compares allowed amounts, not paid amounts.** Paid = allowed minus patient
  responsibility. Comparing payments would flag every high-deductible plan as an
  underpayment.
- **Groups by payer, plan class (from CLP06), place of service, procedure, modifiers** —
  so a commercial rate is never compared against a Medicare Advantage rate.
- **Rate-change detection defines the baseline** rather than being derived from it —
  otherwise a large post-cut sample outvotes the original rate and hides the cut.
- **Understates deliberately.** Where detectors disagree, the lower estimate survives.
  Headline is *recoverable*, never gross total.
- **Patient identity is never read** — NM1*QC, DMG and address segments are skipped at
  parse time.

### Validation
Against synthetic data with a planted answer key: **25 of 25 defects found,
dollar-for-dollar, zero false positives.** Tests match finding-by-finding on claim ID
rather than on totals.

### Status
Built and tested against synthetic data only. **It has never seen a real clearinghouse
file.** Next step is running my father's practice files, pending BAA and compliance
setup.

### The economics that killed it as a standalone business
- ~1.2% of collections is typically underpaid.
- A practice collecting $2M has ~$24,000/year of leakage; ~$15,600 recovered;
  **my share ~$6,000–16,000/year.**
- 50 prospects → ~20 clients → roughly **$120,000/year**. A job, not a company.
- **Still worth testing** — it's built, it costs a weekend, and it validates whether the
  network converts.
